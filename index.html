Knowledge Base

Frequently Asked Questions

Evidence-based answers to key questions about progressive overload, nutrition, and training frequency.

What is Progressive Overload and why is it essential? ▼
Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed upon the musculoskeletal and nervous systems during exercise. To stimulate continuous muscle hypertrophy and neural adaptation, you must systematically increase weight lifted, reps performed, or overall training volume over successive sessions.
How accurate is the Mifflin-St Jeor TDEE formula? ▼
Clinical studies show that the Mifflin-St Jeor equation predicts Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR) within 10% of indirect calorimetry in the majority of individuals, making it the most validated non-laboratory predictive equation available today.
How much protein do I need to optimize muscle growth? ▼
Current sports science literature (such as Morton et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine) indicates that an intake of 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight (0.7 to 1.0g per pound) is optimal for maximizing muscle protein synthesis in resistance-trained athletes.
Should I train to muscular failure on every set? ▼
Training to absolute concentric failure on every compound set generates excessive central nervous system fatigue and increases joint strain. Research demonstrates that training with 1 to 3 Reps in Reserve (RIR) produces equivalent hypertrophy while dramatically improving recovery between workouts.
Can I use FitTrack Pro offline without an internet connection? ▼
Yes! All workouts, calculators, personal records, and meal entries are stored locally on your device via HTML5 LocalStorage. The audio synthesizer uses the native Web Audio API and does not require streaming external audio files.
